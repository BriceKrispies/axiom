Procedural Mesh Crucible

Eight concentric rings of walking dachshunds — 104 of them, packed nose to tail, each ring turning against both of its neighbours — on generated ground. Every visible triangle below was produced by an axiom-mesh-ops operator and validated by axiom-mesh: there is no imported geometry in this app and no hand-typed vertex array. The ground is a meshed height array; each dog is a lofted torso, tapered swept limbs, an icosphere skull and rounded-box paws, cut at the joints into a 23-bone rig and walked by distance-driven inverse kinematics.

The animal is 3.05× as long as it is tall (2.40 units nose to tail on a 0.79-unit height) with forelegs a sixth of its length — and that one ratio sizes the rest of the app. A longer body bulges further off a curve, so the radial pitch and the innermost radius fall out of it; shorter legs have less absolute reach, so the stride, the crouch and the terrain the feet may follow fall out of it too. The tables below are all downstream of the dog.

All 104 are the same 23 meshes in one of 18 shared coats. The geometry is uploaded once and drawn 2393 times as instances, and those instances collapse into 415 draw calls — one per (bone mesh, coat) pair, plus the terrain. Both numbers are the point: adding a dog costs a transform and a palette index, never a vertex and never a draw call.

Six rings and sixteen dogs fewer than the standard-proportioned dog this field used to hold, and both losses are the same arithmetic: a 24-unit body needs 25.5 units of arc apiece where a 21-unit one needed 22.5, and it bulges further off every curve, so the radial pitch had to widen too. Tightening the nose-to-tail gap does not buy them back — at a 1.2-unit gap the rounding lands on the same 104 dogs, and at 1.0 the innermost ring rounds up to seven animals with only 23.3 units of arc each, which is less than the dog is long.

How the field is derived

QuantityWhere it comes from
dogs per ringThe ring's circumference divided by the room one dog needs — a 24.0-unit body plus a 1.5-unit gap = 25.5 units — rounded to a whole animal. Nothing is typed; move a radius and the ring re-populates itself.
radial pitch — 7.75Bounded by the dog's width (3.84), not its length: a dog lies along its ring. The one correction is that a rigid 24-unit body standing on a circle has its nose and tail outside it, by √(R² + 12²) − R — 2.64 units at radius 26, 0.99 at radius 72.5. So 7.75 ≥ 3.84 + 2.64 everywhere, leaving 1.27 to 2.92 units of clear air between neighbouring chains. Stretching the dog made the bulge worse (it was 2.12) and narrowing it paid for that: a long low dog is a narrow one, even with stouter leg tubes than before.
innermost — 26A floor, not a preference. The bulge above grows as the curve tightens (3.63 at radius 18, 5.62 at 10) and at radius 12 the body is longer than the circle it stands on. 26 is where the gait is tuned, and it had to be re-earned: the longer body pushed the shoulder further outside the circle its own paw is planted on, while the leg absorbing that offset halved. What pays for both is that a dachshund's leg is authored bent, so it stands at 74% of its own reach instead of 105% and has real swing budget without being folded into the ground. The suite measures every limb of every dog over more than a lap — worst reach 86%.
outermost — 80.25The terrain's top surface is a 192×192 square (half-extent 96) with the skirt hanging straight down from its border, so the usable ground is the inscribed disc of radius 96. An outermost dog reaches ~83.1 from the origin, leaving 12.9 units — half a dog's length — of clear ground before the rim. It came in from 82: the rule is stated in dog-lengths, so a longer dog demands a wider verge at the same moment its bulge pushes it outward.
stride — 4.8…5.2Short legs have less absolute reach, so the stride came down with them — 5.2 units against the old 8.2, on a body that is longer. Relative to its own length the dachshund's step is 56% of what the standard dog's was, and the travel per tick did not change, so the cycle runs 1.6× faster over the ground: a step every 8.4 ticks rather than 13.2. Short, quick, busy steps are not a separate styling choice; they are what falls out of halving the legs.
body pitchThe one genuinely new mechanism. A rigid body held level makes every leg span the whole of the terrain's roll across the wheelbase — affordable on a 7-unit wheelbase with 5.5-unit legs, and not on a 10-unit wheelbase with 3.7-unit ones: it clamped every front leg at 108% of its length, on the widest rings as badly as the tightest. The body now lies along the slope measured at ±½ wheelbase, which lifts each hip by very nearly what the ground under its own foot has risen, leaving the legs only the terrain's curvature to absorb.
windingThe ring's index parity, and nothing else: reversing the authored parameter direction reverses the tangent, which reverses the facing. There is no separate "turn the dogs around" step, so neighbouring rings cannot accidentally agree.

Colour, and why there are only 18 of them

ConcernWhat is done
the constraintDraws batch on the (mesh, material) pair, and a draw's colour reaches the GPU only through its material — the per-instance colour[4] in the instance stream is filled from the material the draw names. One coat per dog would therefore be 23 × 104 = 2392 single-instance draw calls, which is instancing thrown away. (The engine-tier fix — a per-renderable tint excluded from the batch key — is described in the app's NOTES.md; it was recorded, not made.)
the palette18 hues, registered once and shared by the whole field, so the draw-call count is at most 23 × 18 + 1 = 415 for any crowd size. The eight rings as laid out wear every one of the 18, so the real number is exactly 415 — and it would still be 415 with ten times the dogs.
readabilityThe palette is two interleaved combs of 9 hues; a ring uses the comb its index parity names. Two dogs adjacent along a ring are therefore at least 40° apart in hue, and a dog on one ring and any dog on a neighbouring ring at least 20° — which matters more now that every ring turns the same way: neighbouring dogs hold their relative alignment instead of sliding past each other, so a shared hue would sit side by side indefinitely.
gaitOne trot, one rig, one set of legs. A dog's place in its chain is a fixed arc-length offset, which spaces the ring and puts every dog at a different point in the stride — the legs run as a wave rather than stamping in lockstep. Every paw is planted: during stance its world position is a function of the step number alone, so it does not slide while the body travels over it.

GestureWhat it does

Debug views

QueryWhat it shows
?detail=base|dense|coarseRe-tessellates the whole scene — the terrain grid, every swept limb's stations and rings, the skull's subdivisions. Only tessellation counts change; the authoring is identical, and so is the layout of all eight rings.
?view=flatEvery mesh re-normalled with generate_flat_normals: unwelded, one normal per triangle. Facets appear on the swept and lathed surfaces.
?view=smoothEvery mesh re-normalled with generate_normals (area-weighted). Compare against the operators' own normals in the default view.
?view=normalsColour by normal. Each vertex's UV is replaced by its normal's (x, y) mapped into 0..1 and sampled from an app-authored chart texture: red rises with +X, green with +Y, blue falls off toward the rim. The two z-hemispheres share a chart cell.

The dogs are drawn as rigid bones rather than skinned geometry on purpose: the WebGL2 fallback this page must survive on has no vertex-stage storage buffers and draws no skinned geometry at all, whereas a re-authored instance transform is an ordinary instanced draw. Wireframe and a per-object UV checker are not available: the renderer has one triangle-list pipeline with no line topology, and a checker overlay would need a second UV set alongside the operator-authored one. Both are recorded as limitations in the app's NOTES.md rather than worked around in the renderer. The presentation surface is sized once, to the device, at startup — a rotation or window resize reloads the page, for the reason recorded there too.
