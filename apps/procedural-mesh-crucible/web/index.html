Procedural Mesh Crucible

Two counter-rotating rings of walking dogs, on generated ground. Every visible triangle below was produced by an axiom-mesh-ops operator and validated by axiom-mesh: there is no imported geometry in this app and no hand-typed vertex array. The ground is a meshed height array; each dog is a lofted torso, tapered swept limbs, an icosphere skull and rounded-box paws, cut at the joints into a 23-bone rig and walked by distance-driven inverse kinematics.

The outer ring holds 12 dogs walking anticlockwise; the inner ring holds 7 walking the other way. All 19 are the same 23 meshes — the geometry is uploaded once and drawn 437 times as instances, so a longer chain costs transforms and colours, never vertices.

The two rings

RingWhat it is
outer — 12 dogsRadius 46, walked anticlockwise seen from above. 289 units of ring ÷ (a 21-unit dog + a 3-unit gap) = 12 walkers, nose to tail.
inner — 7 dogsRadius 26, walked clockwise — the opposite way round. 163 units ÷ 24 = 7 walkers.
colourEach ring sweeps the full hue circle once, the inner one half a turn out of phase, so no two neighbours anywhere share a colour.
gaitOne trot, one rig, one set of legs. A dog's place in its chain is a fixed arc-length offset, which spaces the ring and puts every dog at a different point in the stride — the legs run as a wave rather than stamping in lockstep. Every paw is planted: during stance its world position is a function of the step number alone, so it does not slide while the body travels over it.

Camera

GestureWhat it does
drag (1 finger / left mouse)Orbit. Yaw is unbounded; pitch stops ~6.9° short of each pole, so the horizon never flips.
pinch (2 fingers)Zoom, from the change in the contacts' separation. The same move also pans by their midpoint's travel.
wheelZoom, exponentially — a sixth of the distance per notch, so it feels the same at 4 units out and at 400.
two-finger drag · right-drag · shift-dragPan. The world distance scales with the orbit distance, so a point under the pointer stays under it.

Debug views

QueryWhat it shows
?detail=base|dense|coarseRe-tessellates the whole scene — the terrain grid, every swept limb's stations and rings, the skull's subdivisions. Only tessellation counts change; the authoring is identical, and so is the layout of both rings.
?view=flatEvery mesh re-normalled with generate_flat_normals: unwelded, one normal per triangle. Facets appear on the swept and lathed surfaces.
?view=smoothEvery mesh re-normalled with generate_normals (area-weighted). Compare against the operators' own normals in the default view.
?view=normalsColour by normal. Each vertex's UV is replaced by its normal's (x, y) mapped into 0..1 and sampled from an app-authored chart texture: red rises with +X, green with +Y, blue falls off toward the rim. The two z-hemispheres share a chart cell.

The dogs are drawn as rigid bones rather than skinned geometry on purpose: the WebGL2 fallback this page must survive on has no vertex-stage storage buffers and draws no skinned geometry at all, whereas a re-authored instance transform is an ordinary instanced draw. Wireframe and a per-object UV checker are not available: the renderer has one triangle-list pipeline with no line topology, and a checker overlay would need a second UV set alongside the operator-authored one. Both are recorded as limitations in the app's NOTES.md rather than worked around in the renderer. The presentation surface is sized once, to the device, at startup — a rotation or window resize reloads the page, for the reason recorded there too.
