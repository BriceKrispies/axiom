Procedural Mesh Crucible

Eight concentric rings of walking dogs — 120 of them, packed nose to tail, each ring turning against both of its neighbours — on generated ground. Every visible triangle below was produced by an axiom-mesh-ops operator and validated by axiom-mesh: there is no imported geometry in this app and no hand-typed vertex array. The ground is a meshed height array; each dog is a lofted torso, tapered swept limbs, an icosphere skull and rounded-box paws, cut at the joints into a 23-bone rig and walked by distance-driven inverse kinematics.

All 120 are the same 23 meshes in one of 18 shared coats. The geometry is uploaded once and drawn 2761 times as instances, and those instances collapse into 392 draw calls — one per (bone mesh, coat) pair, plus the terrain. Both numbers are the point: adding a dog costs a transform and a palette index, never a vertex and never a draw call.

How the field is derived

QuantityWhere it comes from
dogs per ringThe ring's circumference divided by the room one dog needs — a 21.0-unit body plus a 1.5-unit gap = 22.5 units — rounded to a whole animal. Nothing is typed; move a radius and the ring re-populates itself.
radial pitch — 8.0Bounded by the dog's width (4.24), not its length: a dog lies along its ring. The one correction is that a rigid 21-unit body standing on a circle has its nose and tail outside it, by √(R² + 10.5²) − R — 2.12 units at radius 26, 0.74 at radius 74. So 8.0 ≥ 4.24 + 2.12 everywhere, leaving 1.7 to 3.0 units of clear air between neighbouring chains.
innermost — 26A floor, not a preference. The bulge above grows as the curve tightens (3.06 at radius 18, 5.51 at 10) and at radius 10.5 the body is longer than the circle it stands on. 26 is where the gait is tuned — the shortened stride and deepened crouch were sized for exactly this curve, and the test suite measures every limb of every dog over more than a lap (worst reach: 92% of the leg's length). A tighter ring is a re-tuned gait, not a smaller constant.
outermost — 82The terrain's top surface is a 192×192 square (half-extent 96) with the skirt hanging straight down from its border, so the usable ground is the inscribed disc of radius 96. An outermost dog reaches ~84.9 from the origin, leaving 11.2 units — half a dog's length — of clear ground before the rim.
windingThe ring's index parity, and nothing else: reversing the authored parameter direction reverses the tangent, which reverses the facing. There is no separate "turn the dogs around" step, so neighbouring rings cannot accidentally agree.

Colour, and why there are only 18 of them

ConcernWhat is done
the constraintDraws batch on the (mesh, material) pair, and a draw's colour reaches the GPU only through its material — the per-instance colour[4] in the instance stream is filled from the material the draw names. One coat per dog would therefore be 23 × 120 = 2760 single-instance draw calls, which is instancing thrown away. (The engine-tier fix — a per-renderable tint excluded from the batch key — is described in the app's NOTES.md; it was recorded, not made.)
the palette18 hues, registered once and shared by the whole field, so the draw-call count is at most 23 × 18 + 1 = 415 for any crowd size. The field as laid out wears 17 of the 18, so the real number is 392 — fewer draw calls than the two-ring version it replaced, with six times the dogs.
readabilityThe palette is two interleaved combs of 9 hues; a ring uses the comb its index parity names. Two dogs adjacent along a ring are therefore at least 40° apart in hue, and a dog on one ring and any dog on a neighbouring ring at least 20° — which matters because neighbouring rings counter-rotate, so every relative alignment happens.
gaitOne trot, one rig, one set of legs. A dog's place in its chain is a fixed arc-length offset, which spaces the ring and puts every dog at a different point in the stride — the legs run as a wave rather than stamping in lockstep. Every paw is planted: during stance its world position is a function of the step number alone, so it does not slide while the body travels over it.

Camera

GestureWhat it does
drag (1 finger / left mouse)Orbit. Yaw is unbounded; pitch stops ~6.9° short of each pole, so the horizon never flips.
pinch (2 fingers)Zoom, from the change in the contacts' separation. The same move also pans by their midpoint's travel.
wheelZoom, exponentially — a sixth of the distance per notch, so it feels the same at 4 units out and at 400.
two-finger drag · right-drag · shift-dragPan. The world distance scales with the orbit distance, so a point under the pointer stays under it.

Debug views

QueryWhat it shows
?detail=base|dense|coarseRe-tessellates the whole scene — the terrain grid, every swept limb's stations and rings, the skull's subdivisions. Only tessellation counts change; the authoring is identical, and so is the layout of all eight rings.
?view=flatEvery mesh re-normalled with generate_flat_normals: unwelded, one normal per triangle. Facets appear on the swept and lathed surfaces.
?view=smoothEvery mesh re-normalled with generate_normals (area-weighted). Compare against the operators' own normals in the default view.
?view=normalsColour by normal. Each vertex's UV is replaced by its normal's (x, y) mapped into 0..1 and sampled from an app-authored chart texture: red rises with +X, green with +Y, blue falls off toward the rim. The two z-hemispheres share a chart cell.

The dogs are drawn as rigid bones rather than skinned geometry on purpose: the WebGL2 fallback this page must survive on has no vertex-stage storage buffers and draws no skinned geometry at all, whereas a re-authored instance transform is an ordinary instanced draw. Wireframe and a per-object UV checker are not available: the renderer has one triangle-list pipeline with no line topology, and a checker overlay would need a second UV set alongside the operator-authored one. Both are recorded as limitations in the app's NOTES.md rather than worked around in the renderer. The presentation surface is sized once, to the device, at startup — a rotation or window resize reloads the page, for the reason recorded there too.
